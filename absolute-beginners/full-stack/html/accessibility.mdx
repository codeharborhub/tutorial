In the real world, we build ramps for wheelchairs and put braille on elevator buttons. On the web, we do the same thing! 

**Web Accessibility** means making sure your website can be used by **everyone**, including people who:
* Are blind or have low vision.
* Cannot use a mouse (they use only a keyboard).
* Are colorblind.
* Have temporary injuries (like a broken arm).

## 1. The "Screen Reader" Perspective

A screen reader is software that reads the code of a website out loud. Imagine trying to use your favorite app with your eyes closed—that is how many people experience your work.

### How to help the Screen Reader:
* **Use Semantic Tags:** As we learned, using `<header>` instead of a `<div>` tells the reader exactly where the top of the page is.
* **The "Alt" Rule:** Always describe your images. 
    * ❌ `<img src="dog.jpg">` → The reader just says "Image."
    * ✅ `<img src="dog.jpg" alt="A golden retriever playing with a ball">` → The reader describes the scene.

## 2. The Keyboard Test

Some users cannot use a mouse due to motor disabilities. They navigate the web using the **Tab** key.

### How to be Keyboard Friendly:
1. **Never remove the "Focus Outline":** You know that blue box that appears around a button when you click it? That tells keyboard users where they are. Don't hide it!
2. **Use Buttons for Actions:** If something is clickable, use a `<button>` or `<a>`. If you use a `<div>`, a keyboard user cannot "tab" to it.

## 3. Color and Contrast

If the text color is too similar to the background color, many people won't be able to read it.

| Good Contrast | Bad Contrast |
| :--- | :--- |
| <span style={{backgroundColor: '#000', color: '#fff', padding: '2px'}}>White on Black</span> | <span style={{backgroundColor: '#eee', color: '#ccccccde', padding: '2px'}}>Light Grey on Grey</span> |

**The Golden Rule:** Don't use color *only* to show meaning. 
* ❌ "Click the green button to save." (A colorblind user might see two grey buttons).
* ✅ "Click the green button labeled 'Save'."

## 4. Meaningful Link Text

Avoid using "Click Here" for your links. It gives no context to someone using a screen reader who is skipping from link to link.

* ❌ `To see our prices, <a href="/pricing">click here</a>.`
* ✅ `View our <a href="/pricing">full pricing plans</a>.`

## 🍱 5. The "a11y" Checklist for Beginners

<Tabs>
  <TabItem value="check" label="✅ Quick Checklist" default>
    <ul>
      <li>Every image has an <code>alt</code> description.</li>
      <li>The page has a clear <code>&lt;h1&gt;</code> heading.</li>
      <li>Form inputs have <code>&lt;label&gt;</code> tags.</li>
      <li>Text is large enough to read (at least 16px).</li>
      <li>The language is set: <code>&lt;html lang="en"&gt;</code>.</li>
    </ul>
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem value="tools" label="🛠️ Helpful Tools">
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Lighthouse:</strong> Built into Chrome DevTools. It gives you an accessibility score out of 100.</li>
      <li><strong>WAVE:</strong> A browser extension that points out exactly what to fix on your page.</li>
    </ul>
  </TabItem>
</Tabs>

::::tip Why it matters for your career
Big companies (like Google, Amazon, and Netflix) prioritize accessibility because it’s the law in many countries and it increases their total number of users. Learning this now makes you a much more valuable developer!


:::info Summary
Accessibility isn't about following hard rules; it's about **empathy**. Build your sites so that everyone, regardless of their situation, can enjoy what you've created.
:::
